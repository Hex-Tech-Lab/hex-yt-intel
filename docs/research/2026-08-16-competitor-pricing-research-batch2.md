Researched 2026-08-16 via Exa (`web_search_exa`) and Brave (`brave_web_search`) in parallel, per standing multi-engine directive. This is the SECOND batch of ~25-30 competitors, deliberately steering away from top-of-list tools (Eightify, NoteGPT, Summarize.tech, Notta, etc.) that a parallel agent's first batch was expected to cover, and leaning into niche/newer/less-obvious entrants: creator-intelligence platforms, mind-map generators, chapter generators, comment analyzers, and credit-based utilities. All data pulled directly from vendor pricing pages or vendor-page excerpts surfaced by search; no figures extrapolated from memory. Maturity/funding data is largely NOT publicly discoverable for these smaller tools — noted per row rather than guessed.

## Notes on Coverage Gaps

- **ytskim** and **TubeSumma**: confirmed credit-based models but exact top-up/credit pack $ prices were not disclosed in the Chrome Web Store listings or accessible pricing pages within this search pass — flagged rather than guessed.
- **ChapterMe/ChapterGPT**: pricing page exists (chapterme.co) but the specific dollar figures for Premium/Enterprise weren't captured in the search snippet returned; would need a direct page fetch to confirm exact numbers.
- Funding/company-age data was essentially undiscoverable for nearly every tool in this batch — these read overwhelmingly as solo-founder or very-small-team bootstrapped products (same category as hex-yt-intel itself), not VC-backed. Where a tool explicitly stated scale (e.g., TubeOnAI's "30,000+ users") that's noted; otherwise assume unverified/small.

## Synthesis

1. **Credit-based pricing is now the dominant mechanic in this second tier of competitors**, more so than flat "summaries/month" caps — roughly half the tools surveyed (Quiki.io, Tube-U, YoTrends, CommentScope, Dig.ai, MindMap AI, Y2Map, Chapters360, CVI, TubeSumma, ytskim) use credits either as the sole pricing unit or layered on top of subscription tiers. Several combine **both** a subscription with included monthly credits AND a standalone pay-as-you-go top-up option (Tube-U, MindMap AI, CommentScope, Dig.ai, AudienceCue) — this hybrid ("subscription base + optional credit top-up") pattern is the single most common variant of the mechanic hex-yt-intel is considering, not pure PAYG-only (Quiki.io, Creator Value Index, Y2Map are the only pure-PAYG-no-subscription examples found).
2. **Priority processing as a paid-tier lever appears repeatedly** (Rundown Max, BriefTube Pro, Tube-U/CommentScope premium credits, Quiki.io Credits plan) — a specific pattern worth flagging since it's cheap to implement and clearly used as a differentiator rather than a hard usage gate.
3. **Refund policy is almost universally unstated** — only TubeOnAI (explicit no-refund, prepaid/cancel-stops-renewal) and MindMap AI (explicit 7-day money-back up to 100 credits used) and Minr (30-day refund, Lifetime-plan-only) state a policy outright. The silent majority appears to default to "cancel anytime, no refund," consistent with SaaS norms, but this is inference, not confirmed per-vendor.
4. **Lifetime / one-time-purchase deals are common among the smaller, newer entrants** as a bootstrapping cash-flow tactic (Minr $249, MindMap AI $157-397 tiered, CommentScope $299, getMindmap $49.99, CVI's per-channel one-time packs) — several explicitly frame it as a limited launch offer ("ends at 1,000 customers"), a pattern directly relevant to a 9-day-launch bootstrapped context.
5. **Price anchoring at the low end has compressed further than typical "first batch" tools** — several niche/newer entrants price entry paid tiers under $5/mo (yTeaser $1.99, Sumbox $4.99, Neumap $2.99, TubeSummary.org $3.25/mo annual, VexaScribe Starter $2/mo), suggesting downward price pressure specifically among newer/smaller players trying to undercut the established mid-tier ($9-20/mo) cluster that dominates the more visible "top 10 lists."
