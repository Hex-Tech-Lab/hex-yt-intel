## Synthesis

**1. Subscription is the dominant model, but the market is visibly fracturing toward usage-based pricing.** Roughly 20 of the 25 competitors use a classic tiered monthly/annual subscription (Free → Paid tier(s), usually 2-4 tiers). But within that majority, the *mechanism gating usage* is shifting hard toward internal credit systems (NoteGPT's dual Basic/Premium quota, Monica's Advanced Credits, Mapify's AI credits, Sider's per-feature dynamic deduction, ConciseGPT's daily/monthly credits) rather than a flat "summaries per day" cap — credits let vendors price differentially by compute cost per action (a YouTube summary costs less than an image generation or premium-model chat) without maintaining separate SKUs.

**2. True pay-as-you-go / one-time credit packs (no subscription at all) are a real but minority pattern — worth taking seriously.** YT Summarizer (#3) is the clearest pure example: no subscription, $9/$19/$49 one-time packs, credits never expire. Gistilo (#8) and Happy Scribe (#22) both layer *optional top-up packs on top of* a subscription rather than replacing it — Gistilo's "+20 hours for $3" purchased credits explicitly roll over indefinitely while subscription-included hours reset monthly, and Happy Scribe charges $0.20/min once the monthly AI-minute allowance is exhausted. Monica (#20) and BibiGPT (#23) both also sell separate advanced-credit/on-demand top-ups alongside subscriptions. This "subscription base + burst top-up" pattern is the closest direct analog to what hex-yt-intel is reportedly considering, and is more common than a pure credits-only model — pure-credits (YT Summarizer) is the outlier, not the norm.

**3. Free tiers are near-universal (23/25), but almost none are "free forever with real capacity."** Nearly every competitor offers *some* no-cost entry point, but it's almost always a thin trial-like cap (1-5 summaries/day, 30 minutes, or a one-time credit grant) rather than a durable free product — Mindgrasp (#17) is the one clear exception with **no free tier at all**, only a 3-day trial. True "free forever, usable at real volume" (Summarize.tech's unlimited-but-rate-limited model) is rare.

**4. Trial policy is inconsistent even within single vendors, and refund policies are almost never stated.** Only 2 of 25 (Recall, YouLearn) explicitly state a refund window (30-day and 7-day respectively) in the sources found; Glasp explicitly states the opposite — "non-refundable" on its pricing page. The overwhelming default is silence on refunds, implying most competitors rely on standard payment-processor (Stripe) dispute handling rather than a published policy — this is a gap hex-yt-intel could differentiate on cheaply by simply publishing an explicit refund policy. Marqly's own pricing pages disagree with each other on trial length (3-day vs 7-day) — a reminder to verify launch copy for internal consistency before shipping.

**5. Price clustering.** Entry-level paid tiers cluster tightly around **$5-10/month** (or $7-20/mo annualized-equivalent) regardless of company size or maturity — this appears to be the de facto market anchor price for "unlimited-ish" individual usage in this category, from scrappy indie tools (Sumbox $4.99, Eightify $4.99 annual) up to funded players (Notta $8.17, Recall $10). Prices above ~$20/mo are reserved for either usage-heavy tiers (high-volume minutes/credits) or adjacent-feature bundling (team seats, multi-format tools like Sider/Monica that aren't YouTube-specific).
